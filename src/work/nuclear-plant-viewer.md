## The problem

The Digital Plant Viewer (DPV) is a web application that gives employees a central, interactive map of a power plant. Constellation saw low adoption across its plants and needed to know whether the cause was the software, the way it was deployed, or both.

## How we studied it

The research was contextual and flexible, so we could follow what we found on site:

1. **Observation** of the plant areas where DPV kiosks were installed.
2. **Intercepts** with operators passing by, asking about their use of the kiosks and of DPV in general.
3. **Semi-structured interviews** with workers and managers to understand what we had observed.
4. **Training observation**, watching DPV sessions with operators who had never used it.
5. **Quick usability tests** on the spot, especially with newer users.

## What we found

**The interface didn't match operators' work.** On tablets and laptops, DPV's navigation was not intuitive, screens were overwhelming and the information architecture was confusing. Newer users struggled most.

**Access was in the wrong places.** Devices varied by plant: some had tablets, some laptops, some only kiosks near the entrances to contaminated areas. Many operators had no personal device and had to walk to a kiosk. But operators plan their day together in briefing rooms, where DPV wasn't available. When they did reach a kiosk, they often met unresponsive software and faulty touch screens.

**Many employees didn't know it existed.** Awareness ranged from regular users, usually close to the design team, to people who had heard of it in training but never used it, to people who had never heard of it. DPV had not been promoted inside the plants, and it took up a small part of large-group training sessions that left little time for questions.

## Recommendations

1. Redesign DPV's information architecture and layout around operators' goals and activities.
2. Fix the kiosk software and touch-screen faults.
3. Install DPV on briefing-room computers, where operators and managers plan their work.
4. Include DPV in the plants' regular internal communications.
5. Replace large-group training with smaller, scenario-based sessions, and give trainers simple observation guidelines so the system keeps improving from real use.
